# Ontology — FILM GRAMMAR
11 categories x 11 curated terms = 121 terms
Format: term — (effect / best use)

## SHOT-SIZE
- extreme-wide-shot — (establish scale, isolation, epic scope)
- wide-shot — (environment context, character in space)
- full-shot — (head-to-toe body, blocking clarity)
- cowboy-shot — (thighs-up, western standoff tension)
- medium-shot — (waist-up dialogue default, natural intimacy)
- medium-close-up — (chest-up, emotional emphasis without intrusion)
- close-up — (face fills frame, raw emotion)
- extreme-close-up — (eyes, lips, detail as abstraction)
- insert-shot — (object detail, narrative information beat)
- over-the-shoulder — (dialogue geometry, perspective anchoring)
- pov-shot — (subjective camera, total audience immersion)

## COVERAGE
- master-shot — (whole scene wide, editorial safety net)
- single — (isolated character, clean dialogue angle)
- two-shot — (paired characters, relationship in frame)
- group-shot — (ensemble blocking, social dynamics)
- reaction-shot — (listener focus, emotion without words)
- reverse-angle — (180-flip, conversational counterpoint)
- dirty-single — (foreground shoulder blur, spatial intimacy)
- walk-and-talk — (moving dialogue, Sorkin-style momentum)
- oner — (unbroken long take, technical virtuosity)
- aerial-coverage — (drone overhead geography, god's-eye view)
- rack-focus — (attention shift within single frame)

## EDIT-CUT
- hard-cut — (instant transition, jolt and urgency)
- smash-cut — (abrupt tonal collision, comedic shock)
- match-cut — (visual rhyme across scenes, elegant continuity)
- jump-cut — (time compression, Godard-style rupture)
- l-cut — (audio leads video, seamless dialogue flow)
- j-cut — (audio precedes image, anticipatory smoothness)
- cross-cut — (parallel action, simultaneous tension)
- cutaway — (b-roll escape, context or editorial patch)
- invisible-cut — (hidden transition, fake single take)
- whip-pan-cut — (blur-masked transition, kinetic energy)
- freeze-frame — (halted motion, reflective punctuation)
